New Year Journaling & Planner Gift Ideas: Starting 2027 with a Good Notebook and a Photo That Takes Photos

With 2027 around the corner, looking for a New Year gift for someone who loves journaling and documenting everyday life? Start with our top pick—Keeper, "a photo that takes photos" made for the same desk as their notebook—alongside a two-days-per-page planner, a slim weekly journal, and a five-year Q&A diary.

As the year winds down, almost everyone feels the quiet urge to reset—pick up a fresh planner, clear off the desk, and think gently about the memories worth keeping in the year ahead.

What turns out to be worth remembering is rarely just big meetings or ambitious resolutions. More often, it's a movie that lingered with you, an unhurried weekend brunch, a sentence underlined in a book, or an afternoon when the sunlight felt especially warm. If you are shopping for a New Year gift welcoming 2027 for a friend who loves stationery and journaling—or for yourself—these four ideas give both words and pictures a quiet place to land.

01

1. Top Pick: Start with Keeper, "A Photo That Takes Photos," for a Paper-Like Visual Diary All Year Long

People who love paper journals usually share two instincts: they gravitate toward warm, matte textures that are easy on the eyes, and they love pairing words with real moments from their day. If you could choose just one memory-keeping gift for 2027, Keeper is our top pick.

Keeper is "A photo that takes photos." Technically, it is a pocket digital camera with a 3.7-inch six-color e-paper screen (480 × 720). Rest it beside a notebook and coffee mug on a wooden desk, and its matte color e-paper display reads with the calm softness of a printed photograph—as though a picture stepped right off the pages of a diary.

  • Capture one frame from your day without pulling out a phone: On the lower-right corner sits a separate small IPS LCD viewfinder (display only, non-touch), paired with a crisp physical round red shutter button on the lower-left. On a walk, at a bookstore, or over brunch, press once to capture a photo independently onto a microSD card—no phone to unlock, no app to open, and zero notification pop-ups breaking your focus.
  • Always visible with no photo paper to keep buying: Keeper does not print paper photos and needs no film refills. Instead, your latest photograph stays displayed on a matte, reflective six-color e-paper screen. Because e-paper draws power only when refreshing the image, that picture stays steadily in view even when powered off.
  • Rides on a bag, stands by your desk, or snaps onto metal surfaces: A lanyard comes in the gift box so it travels effortlessly alongside a daily planner, and its magnetic back snaps neatly onto a study pegboard or kitchen fridge at home. For everyday photography, charging over USB-C takes roughly twice a year (actual battery life varies with how often you shoot, refresh the display, and go online).
  • Exports online on its own for effortless year-end recaps: Keeper connects to the internet by itself to export photos with no cable or phone handoff needed, and also supports receiving photos sent remotely by family or friends along with cloud sync—making it wonderfully easy to look back on an entire year of frames come December.

02

2. Fill the Year Unhurriedly: A Lay-Flat Two-Days-Per-Page Planner

Plenty of people buy a thick page-a-day diary only to stall out by March staring at full empty pages—while tiny monthly calendar boxes barely fit a single thought. A two-days-per-page layout hits the calmest middle ground between the two.

Each day claims just half a page: room at the top for two or three tasks, and space below for one small story, a book you finished, or a favorite dinner, with zero pressure to write an essay. Look for three essentials when picking one out: smooth bleed-resistant paper, thread-bound 180-degree lay-flat binding, and a cover that feels comforting in the hand—the kind of notebook that invites you to pick up a pen the moment it opens.

03

3. A Whole Week at a Glance: A Slim Weeks-Style Weekly Journal

For someone who naturally plans work and life week by week, a slim Weeks-style weekly planner is the carry-everywhere notebook that actually sees daily use.

Open to any spread: the left page lays out Monday through Sunday clearly, while the right page stays an open grid for shopping lists, book quotes, quick meeting notes, or taping in a movie stub or gallery ticket. Featherlight in a tote or coat pocket and dressed in a soft illustrated or nature-inspired cover, it makes pulling out a pen at a café feel like a small pleasure.

04

4. A Slow Gift to Your Future Self: A Five-Year Q&A Diary

Want to keep a diary yet never know how to begin? A five-year "one page per date" Q&A journal lowers the barrier to showing up every night.

Arranged across 365 days, each page prints one light, thoughtful question above five lined spaces for five consecutive years. Two minutes and a couple of sentences before bed is all it takes—and the quiet magic arrives in year two and year three, when turning to the same date reveals instantly what you worried about and what made you smile twelve months earlier. Clothbound in restrained navy or vermilion, it makes an especially meaningful gift heading into 2027.

Three Quick Tips for Choosing New Year Journaling Gifts

  1. Match the layout to how they actually write: Daily or two-days-per-page layouts suit longer reflections, weekly planners fit big-picture organizers, and five-year diaries are easiest to stick with on busy schedules.
  2. Pair written words with real imagery: Notebooks hold thoughts and plans while Keeper keeps the look of the day in sight—sitting side by side on the same desk, they complement each other without competing for attention.
  3. Open the year with one warm gesture: Write a short wish for 2027 on the flyleaf of the notebook, or snap a New Year's Eve coffee photo together on Keeper so it greets them right on the screen.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. For someone who loves journaling, how is Keeper different from an instant-print camera?

Keeper does not print paper photos and never requires buying photo paper refills. It keeps photographs displayed on a 3.7-inch matte six-color e-paper screen (480 × 720) that looks close to printed paper without glowing—making it a calm visual diary right beside notebooks on a desk—while also letting you export digital copies online on its own for archiving.

2. Does Keeper need a smartphone app to shoot? Will it pull me back into phone distractions?

Not at all. Keeper handles framing and shooting completely on its own—just press the physical round red shutter in the lower-left corner to shoot and save to a microSD card, with no phone to unlock and no app to open. It is ideal for anyone trying to document daily life away from notifications.

3. If Keeper sits on my desk displaying a photo all the time, does it need frequent charging?

No. Color e-paper draws power only when changing images, keeping a static photograph visible even when powered off. For everyday photography, charging via USB-C takes roughly twice a year (actual battery life varies with how often you shoot, change images, and go online).

4. Besides photos I take myself, can friends or family send photos remotely to my Keeper?

Yes. Keeper connects to the internet on its own to export photos, and also supports receiving photos sent remotely by family or loved ones along with cloud sync.
